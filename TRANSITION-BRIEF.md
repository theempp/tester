# TRANSITION BRIEF — what the pin hands off to

Raised by the designer 2026-09-15, after the film's staging locked. **Not yet decided.**
The next chat produces four directions and the designer picks one.

## The ask, in the designer's words

At the end of the video the beans cover the whole frame and the camera is falling down with
them. That should transition into something, and that something should carry the scroll
animation on through the site for a while. Their example — not a decision — is a solo object
moving around the frame: centre, to the left, back to centre, to the right, back to centre,
the way lightweight.info moves its wheel.

## Why the handoff is unusually open

The film ends on a FIELD, not a composition. No subject, no focal point — just near-black
bean texture travelling downward. Nothing has to be matched or resolved, so whatever arrives
next can arrive anywhere in frame and own it completely. This is the freest moment on the
page and the best place to spend an idea.

## Constraints any direction must satisfy

**Identity (signed, not reopenable here):** cold instrument over a hot process. Near-black
ground `#0B0B0C`, foreground `#EDEDE8`, hairlines, ember `#C4491F` reserved for the counter
and CTA only. Mono type, `tabular-nums`, uppercase labels. NOT: editorial white-space luxury,
soft spatial product-render air, warm lifestyle-cafe documentary.

**The DEVICE is already spoken for.** The never-resetting counter detaches at the unpin and
docks to the page edge, still counting, for every section below. Any new object shares the
page with it and must not compete with it. Ideally it reinforces it.

**Direction of travel.** The film ends moving DOWN, matching the scroll. Whatever arrives
should either inherit that motion or deliberately break it — and say which.

**Engine budget.** MECHANISM is `pin-film` and TASTE reserves WebGL for one hero object,
never two engines on one pin. A persistent object living below the pin is a real
`engine-decision` reopening — the designer has explicitly reopened it, which is allowed.
Each direction MUST name:
- what engine carries the object (R3F/Three, DOM+CSS, SVG, sprite sheet, a second video)
- whether it changes MECHANISM, and if so to what
- what it costs in assets, since NOTHING 3D exists on disk

**Assets.** No GLB, no model, no object stills. A real 3D object means either generating one
or building it. A DOM/SVG/type-based object costs nothing. That asymmetry should shape the
ranking, not be discovered afterwards.

**The only hero object this business has is the bag** — matte black flat-bottom pouch, flush
brushed-steel valve, struck date. It is also the CTA. An object that IS the bag, moving
through stations and arriving at Buy, is the obvious strong candidate — which is exactly why
at least one direction should argue against it.

## The lightweight.info technique, verified

From `REFERENCE-lightweight.md` (live DOM, 2026-09-15). Techniques only — its wheel, palette
and composition are its identity and are not transferable.

1. A **fixed persistent layer**, not a per-section pin. The object never unmounts; stations
   change its state. `position: fixed` behind the DOM flow.
2. Stations observed: object centred → pushed left with theme inverting dark-to-light →
   left with spec text right → re-posed with a hairline leader line to a callout.
3. Four fixed 9px `+` registration marks at the viewport corners.
4. A fixed full-viewport scroll-timeline instrument.
5. Total runway 17.84vh.

## What the four directions must each contain

- **The idea** — what arrives, and what it does across stations. ~40 words.
- **How it meets the field** — the specific mechanic at the handoff, given the film ends on
  moving texture with no focal point.
- **Engine + MECHANISM impact + asset cost** — one line, honest.
- **What it proves about the brief** — a transition that only looks good is not a direction.
  The business problem is still "I already buy coffee and don't believe yours tastes
  different."

One of the four should be the cheap DOM/type answer that spends nothing, so the expensive
ones have something real to beat.
